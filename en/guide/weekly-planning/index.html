Weekly Planning: A 30-Minute Sunday Review and a Sample Weekly Plan

Last checked 2026-10-17 · Based on Aria 1.1.4 · Written by LIPSON, the maker of Aria.

Key answer You can plan a week in 30 minutes: review last week, choose your top three for this week, block time on the calendar, then split the rest by day. Check in once on Wednesday and look back at the weekend, and next week’s plan gets much easier.

Why plan the week?

If you only plan the day, whatever is urgent that day wins every day. Seeing the whole week lets you set time aside in advance for important but not urgent work, and spot the days when appointments pile up so you can avoid overloading them.

When should I do it, and how long does it take?

Sunday evening or Monday morning, and 30 minutes is enough. At first the review takes the most time, and picking and splitting get quick as you get used to it. Fix the same day and time each week so you don’t skip it.

How do I plan in 4 steps?

  1. Review last week Write down what you finished, what you didn’t and what took longer than expected. The five questions below speed this up.
  2. Pick your top 3 for the week Choose one each from work, study and personal life so you don’t lean in one direction.
  3. Block time on the calendar Look at fixed events first, then give each of the three a one- to two-hour block. See Time blocking for how.
  4. Split the rest by day Spread the remaining tasks across days, and keep one day light as a catch-up day for anything that slips.

What do I look at to review last week?

  1. Of what I finished last week, which one mattered most?
  2. What did I plan but not do, and was the reason time, energy or priorities?
  3. What took longer than expected? Reflect that in next week’s block lengths.
  4. What carries over to next week, and what do I delete outright?
  5. If I changed just one thing next week, what would it be?

How do I fix a plan that slips?

  • A 10-minute check on Wednesday — If one of your three hasn’t started, move it to a Thursday or Friday block and drop one less important thing.
  • Every evening — Move what is left to tomorrow. In Aria, the evening wrap-up’s Reschedule All does it in one go.
  • Tasks that slipped two weeks in a row — They are too big or less important than you thought. Break them into a first action or take them off the list.

FAQ

How do I plan a single day?

Take today’s column from the weekly plan and write fixed events, one must-do, then the rest in order. Five minutes in the morning is enough, and how to order tasks is in the prioritizing guide.

What is the best way to plan well?

Don’t fill the plan completely. Fill about 70% of your available time and leave the rest for backlog and interruptions, and it is easier to rebuild when the plan breaks.

Where can I get a weekly planner template?

As in the example above, three columns beside the day (fixed events, top-3 blocks, routines) are enough, so you can draw it in a notebook. If you use your calendar app’s week view as the planner, work or school events appear on their own and you don’t have to copy them.
