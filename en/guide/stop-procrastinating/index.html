How to Stop Procrastinating: 5 Tools for When You Cannot Start

Last checked 2026-10-17 · Based on Aria 1.1.4 · By LIPSON

Key answer Putting things off often has less to do with laziness than with not knowing where to start, or with dreading the result. Shrinking the first step to two minutes, setting a start time with a timer, and resetting a backlog in one go can help you get moving again. If it goes on for a long time and makes daily life hard, talk to a professional.

Why do we put things off?

The reasons differ from person to person and often mix. Commonly mentioned ones: the task is so big you do not know where to begin, you feel pressure to do it well, it feels boring or the payoff is far away, you have so much to do that choosing is hard, or the deadline is far enough away that it does not feel real. Knowing your reason makes it easier to pick the right tool.

Tap Start Timer on the morning briefing screen to begin right away with the first task. Getting past just the first five minutes often makes it much easier to keep going.

What if a backlog has piled up?

When the list is long, just looking at it is tiring, so you put things off again. Spend 15 minutes and clean it up like this.

  1. 5 minutes: delete first Delete anything that no longer matters and anything someone else already did.
  2. 5 minutes: pick three for this week Leave dates on only the three things that truly must happen this week.
  3. 5 minutes: take dates off the rest Send the rest to Someday. In Aria, use Reschedule all in the evening wrap-up to move them at once.

For rewriting a single to-do line, how to write a to-do list has 10 examples.

How do you set alerts that reduce putting things off?

  • Deadline-stage alerts — set a due date under the due and reminder section at the bottom of the task screen and Aria alerts you at stages as the deadline gets closer.
  • Must-ring alarm — for things you absolutely cannot miss, it rings like an alarm even in silent mode or a Focus. On the Lock Screen you can snooze it for 10 minutes.
  • Location alerts — get reminded when you reach a place, like “when I arrive at the store.”

Too many alerts make you start swiping them away, so set them only on tasks that really need them. Details are in iPhone to-do alarms. Turning daily tasks into a routine so a streak builds up can help too (routine and habit app).

What if you keep putting things off anyway?

You can also revisit whether the goal still fits you, or tell someone you work with how it is going. But if putting things off goes on for a long time and causes real difficulty at work, school or home, or you are struggling emotionally, do not try to handle it alone. Talk to a professional, such as your doctor, a licensed mental health professional, or your school or workplace counseling service. If you are in crisis, contact your local emergency number or crisis line. This guide helps you organize everyday habits and does not replace a medical diagnosis or treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Is procrastination related to ADHD?

Anyone can procrastinate, so putting things off alone cannot tell you whether you have ADHD or any other condition. If trouble with attention or impulse control lasts a long time and affects your daily life, talk to a doctor or other qualified professional. For everyday app settings, see to-do list tips for ADHD.

What if perfectionism makes me put things off?

Change the goal from “do it well” to “start.” Call your first result a draft, set a time cap such as 15 minutes, and stop when it is up. Write the fixes down as a separate task.

What if I put off studying?

Instead of a subject name, write a range like “solve workbook pages 23–25,” and turn on a timer at your start time. Showing the days left until the exam as a D-day makes it easier to plan (study planner).
