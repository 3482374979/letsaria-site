How to Plan Exam Study: A Study Planner App Guide to Exam Day

Last checked 2026-10-17 · Based on Aria 1.1.4 · Written by LIPSON, the maker of Aria.

Key answer Plan exam study by working backward from exam day, then keep a daily routine and a weekly check to re-split anything you fall behind on. Dedicated apps are strong at tracking study hours, while a to-do app is handy for tying together tasks, countdowns and review reminders.

This guide was written by LIPSON, the maker of Aria.

How do I divide the days until the exam?

Fix the exam date first and work backward. With eight weeks left, you could split it like this.

In Aria, turn the exam date into a D-day and the days left show on the dashboard and in Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets. For how to place widgets, see How to add a to-do widget to your iPhone.

How do I write the daily study list?

Write by amount, not by time. “Unit 2 concepts plus 20 practice questions” is easier to judge as done than “study 3 hours.” Three to five lines a day is plenty.

  • Read unit 2 concepts (pp. 40-58)
  • 20 practice questions from unit 2
  • Redo yesterday’s wrong answers
  • Review unit 1 (day 3)

How do I keep a study routine going?

Starting at the same time in the same place is the biggest help. In Aria you can turn steps like “tidy desk 3 min, check today’s list 2 min, first subject 50 min” into a routine and set an Estimate for each step. Then Follow Along guides you through each step with a timer and a streak builds up. For a single task, tap Start Timer and the time left shows on your Lock Screen. For focus rhythms, see the Pomodoro Technique.

How do I set up review reminders?

Many people review the next day, after 3 days and after 7 days. It is not a rule, so adjust it to the subject.

How do I re-split material I’ve fallen behind on?

  • On Sunday, compare this week’s plan with what really happened. Aria’s weekly briefing looks back at the week’s completions and routines.
  • Spread the backlog across the weeks left, but don’t cram it into one week.
  • If you slip two weeks in a row, cut the daily amount or lower your target number of passes.
  • Each evening, move what you didn’t finish with Reschedule All so tomorrow morning starts with a clean list.

To re-plan week by week, see Weekly planning.

How do I use it with a study-hours tracker?

Aria has no study-hours tracking, study-time rankings or study groups. If you want to log hours, measure them in a study timer app and keep what to study and when (daily list, review dates, D-day) in Aria, so the two don’t overlap.

FAQ

What makes a good study planner app?

It depends on what you need. If logging hours and rankings keep you motivated, a study-time tracker fits. If not missing material, review dates and the exam countdown comes first, a to-do app fits. You can use both.

What if my study plan keeps slipping?

Your daily list is probably too long. Cut the daily amount to 70-80% of what you plan now, and keep one day a week just for catching up. If it still slips, lower your target number of passes.

How should I set review intervals?

The 1, 3 and 7 days in this guide are a common example. They vary by subject and time left, so try them for about two weeks and shorten the gaps for subjects you don’t remember well.
